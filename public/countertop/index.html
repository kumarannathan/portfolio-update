Countertop is a quiet menu bar app for macOS. When a download finishes, it moves the file into a folder that matches what it is. That's the whole app.

How it works

Your Downloads folder fills up like a kitchen counter. Countertop wipes it down.

01

Download something

Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge, Opera, anything. Countertop waits until the download is actually finished before it touches the file.

02

It gets sorted

Images to Images, installers to Applications, .fbx to 3D. A moment later it's in ~/Desktop/Countertop, in the right folder.

03

Put it back if you want

Every move shows up in Activity with Show in Finder and Put Back. Nothing is ever overwritten.

Stays out of your way

Built to be forgotten about. It lives in the menu bar and does one thing well.

Waits for downloads to finish

Partial files (.crdownload, .download, .part) are left alone until they're complete.

Never overwrites anything

If photo.jpg already exists, the new one becomes photo 2.jpg. If a file can't be moved, it stays put and Countertop tells you why.

Your rules

Send any extension or name anywhere: .blend to Blender Assets, .rbxl to Roblox, anything named "Screenshot" to Screenshots. Make your own categories.

Undo, always

Activity lists every move. One click puts a file back where it came from.

Quiet

Groups notifications when several downloads land together. Pause it any time from the menu bar.

Private

No account, no analytics, no cloud. The app runs in the macOS sandbox with no network access.

A look around

Overview, Files, Activity, Rules, Folders and Settings. Most days you'll only open the menu bar panel.

Nothing leaves your Mac

Countertop collects no data. There is no account, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no server.

  • Your files are moved between folders on your Mac. Never uploaded.
  • Settings, rules and activity history live in the app's sandbox container.
  • The app itself has no network access. The only request is a once-a-day update check to GitHub, made by Sparkle's sandboxed downloader, and you can turn it off.
  • It never asks for Full Disk Access, Accessibility, or screen recording.

Install

Download the .dmg, drag Countertop into Applications, open it. Setup is three clicks.

"Apple could not verify Countertop is free of malware" — what to do

Countertop isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer membership), so macOS shows this once. It's open source, and every release ships a .sha256 you can check.

  1. Click Done.
  2. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll to "Countertop was blocked to protect your Mac."
  3. Click Open Anyway, confirm with your password, then Open Anyway again.

Or from the Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Countertop.app
